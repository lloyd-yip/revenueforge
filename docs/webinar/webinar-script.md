# RevenueForge Webinar — Master Skeleton

> **Status:** Skeleton / blueprint stage. NOT the final spoken script.
> **Purpose of this file:** Persist the webinar structure + section-level detail so it survives compaction. We flesh out section-by-section later (deep-dive passes produce the actual spoken copy).
> **Audience:** Cold B2B founders ($3M+), pulled in via the organic webinar funnel.
> **Format:** Live / live-recurring.
> **Primary CTA:** Book the free **Revenue Intelligence Audit** (the call *is* the audit → free gap report).
> **Positioning:** Fractional **CAIRO** (Chief AI *Revenue* Officer) — embedded operator, not a vendor.
> **Last updated:** 2026-06-04

## STRUCTURAL SOURCE — what we borrowed vs. what's ours

**Borrowed from the proven QS "Mechanism" webinar (structure only):**
- Interactive open (chat engagement, "what would make this a win for you?")
- Core-question hook + inflection-point framing
- Credibility via *real* proof (results + screenshots), not claims
- Problem diagnosis that collapses 3 surface problems into ONE root cause ("you don't have a system")
- 10,000-ft system frame BEFORE any deep dive
- Deep-dive sections, each with: a clear before/after, a case study with numbers, and a planted mid-CTA
- Summary-of-process recap
- Logistics block (timeline / energy / cost) answering objections out loud
- Cost-of-inaction section that reframes the risk
- Final CTA → live Q&A with the booking link held on screen
- **Multiple soft mid-CTAs** throughout (not just one close at the end)

**100% RevenueForge (NOT borrowed):**
- The narrative: AI as *expansion* (do valuable work you couldn't before), not substitution
- The "method" wrapper: Diagnose → Build → Adopt → Compound
- The 6-machine use-case walk as the proof engine
- The offer: ongoing embedded fractional CAIRO (a RETAINER — opposite of QS's "build it & you own it, not an agency")
- Proof = **dogfounding** (RevenueForge ran the method on its own revenue function) until real client case studies exist

---

## KEY STRUCTURAL DECISION (flag for Lloyd)

**QS sells ONE linear 4-step funnel.** RevenueForge sells a **portfolio of machines** unified by a **method**. So the deep-dive can't be "4 sequential steps" — it's the **6-machine walk**, wrapped by a 4-beat method so it feels like a system, not a tool list:

- **DIAGNOSE** → the Revenue Intelligence Audit (find where revenue leaks)
- **BUILD** → deploy the specific machine(s) — *this is where the 6 use cases live, as proof*
- **ADOPT** → train the team so it sticks after we build it
- **COMPOUND** → re-diagnose, build the next machine; revenue & profit *per employee* climbs

This 4-beat method also encodes the locked engagement model (operator-anchored, audit-as-multi-machine-converter, Rule-of-3 expansion). **Open question:** do we surface the 4-beat method explicitly on a slide, or keep it implicit and let the 6 machines carry it? (Recommendation: surface it — it's what turns "tools" into "system.")

### 0. OPEN — interactive warm-up
*Purpose: lower skepticism, build live energy, set the interactive contract.*
- **0.1 Welcome / wait for joiners** — "great to have you, give it 30s as people join; drop in chat where you're dialing in from."
- **0.2 Housekeeping** — keep it interactive, use the chat; Q&A at the end for your specific situation.
- **0.3 The win-question** — "What would make the next 40 minutes a win for you?" (primes them to name their own pain → the machines answer it later).
- **Title slide** — a bold revenue-outcome headline (the hook claim), NOT a soft "how to use AI" line. **[HOOK CLAIM TBD — see 1.1]**

### 1. THE HOOK — bold claim → shift → trap → framing
*Purpose: open on a bold revenue claim → name the shift → expose the trap → land the core question that frames the session.*
- **1.1 The bold claim + the shift** — lead with the outcome in money and leverage, grounded in "the industry has changed." Grounding line: *"We're living through the biggest jump in business leverage since the internet — measured in months, not decades. Teenagers are building nine-figure companies from their bedrooms — not because they're smarter than you, but because they started with leverage that didn't exist a few years ago."* Then the de-risking pivot: *"I'm not here to promise you'll be the next billion-dollar teenager. You've got a real business with real complexity. I'm here to show you the same leverage those companies run on can be pointed at YOUR revenue engine — double your close rate, double your lead flow, cut your cost to serve — without losing a year figuring it out."* **[HOOK CLAIM TBD — Lloyd finalizes the exact bold claim/numbers. Candidates: (1) 3x-output-no-added-headcount; (2) triple close rate / double lead volume / halve cost-to-serve; (3) leverage-that-didn't-exist-10-yrs-ago. Bold but not cliché — anchor to a mechanism, ground immediately via 1.4.] [VERIFY teen-startup names/numbers (Aaru, Instant, etc.) before any slide — that source is an AI summary; safer to say "teenagers building nine-figure companies" than to stake credibility on unverified specifics.]**
- **1.2 The trap** — even though that's possible, most companies don't know how to actually do it: they ignore it, drown in 40 tools, or bolt on a toy (ChatGPT, a chatbot) that never touches revenue.
- **1.3 The core question (the framing)** — the real question today answers: *"How do you actually get AI to run your revenue engine?"*
- **1.4 The anti-hype stake** — not theory — the actual machines running live on my own pipeline. Mine.
- **1.5 What this is / isn't** — NOT "how to use AI tools." How to build a system that drives revenue and compounds without you babysitting it.

### 2. CREDIBILITY — prove the hook is real *(moved up, right after the hook)*
*Purpose: neutralize "that's hype" the instant it peaks — by showing you already did it, fast, with evidence.*
- **2.1 The bridge** — "That claim I just made? Here's why it's not hype — I did exactly this in my own business."
- **2.2 Who I am** — serial founder (started multiple companies); currently run a multimillion-dollar marketing agency that I turned into an AI-first business — the whole org.
- **2.3 The proof metrics (our own org, last 6 months)** — doubled our close rate · +30% speed-to-close · doubled LTV:CAC.
- **2.4 The scale line** — scaled to ~$300K/month.
- **2.5 Show the evidence** — quick dashboard screenshots. Flash the proof, don't dwell.
- *(Note: leading with "marketing agency" invites the "so you're an agency?" reaction — brain rebuttal O13 covers it: diagnosis + owning the number is the seam an agency lacks. Just be aware.)*

### 3. AGENDA + WHAT TO STAY FOR *(separated out of credibility)*
*Purpose: set the session roadmap + the reason to stay to the end.*
- **3.1 Agenda** — (1) why most B2B founders are stuck, (2) the machines that fix it, (3) how to find out which ones you need.
- **3.2 Stay-to-end bonus** — gift TBD; only include if we're actually offering one.

### 4. WHO IT'S FOR — fit check + founder read *(this section now carries the macro/emotional problem)*
*Purpose: qualify the right buyer AND make them feel deeply seen. Spine = "the leverage is real but locked away from you, and the clock is ticking."*
- **4.1 Who this is for** — B2B, ~$3M+, real sales team or high-ticket offer, founder-led, real revenue motion already working (we amplify, we don't manufacture from zero).
- **4.2 The reality (the empathy spine):**
  - "You already know the leverage is real. You've watched companies scale in a year or two what used to take twenty. You know AI could 5x, maybe 10x this thing."
  - "But you're maxed out. You already work a lot. You've got responsibilities that break if you drop them — you can't take a month off to go learn to vibe-code." *(key-man risk as texture — one line, not the spine.)*
  - "So the two obvious doors are both shut. Learning to build this yourself is a year you don't have — and maybe not your zone. And hiring someone who genuinely can? Slow, expensive, a real risk — you're not even sure you'd know how to manage them."
  - "So the leverage just… sits there. Technically available, practically out of reach. And revenue keeps growing the old, hard way — on your back."
  - **The turn (opportunity cost / displacement):** "And here's the uncomfortable part. A wave of AI-native companies is starting from zero with this baked in from day one. For them it's easy — no legacy, no habits to unwind. For you it's harder, precisely because you've already been around, already tied to daily operations. So the real cost isn't missing some upside — it's that the business you built the old way might not work in five years, against people operating at a speed and cost you can't match."
- **4.3 Who it's NOT for** — "If you're just starting out, pre-revenue, still figuring out your offer — this is too early. File it away, but you're not who I built this for."
- *(Pacing note: 4.2's opportunity-cost turn is a haymaker landed early. Watch for redundancy with §11 Cost of Inaction — decide later whether §11 trims to avoid repeating it.)*

### 5. AREAS YOU COULD HYPER-OPTIMIZE — the sub-agenda of gaps *(replaces the old tactical "Problem" section)*
*Purpose: preview a handful of fixable gaps (app-agnostic), spark "I didn't know that was fixable," let the audience self-select. NO apps named yet — those get demoed in the deep-dive (§7).*
- **5.1 The bridge** — "So AI could be huge leverage for you. In what way? Let me show you."
- **5.2 The frame (sub-agenda)** — "I want to walk you through a handful of areas in your business you could hyper-optimize — that you might not even realize ARE optimizable yet. These aren't all of them. They're just some of the most common gaps that quietly cost you money — the kind that plug easily, if you know how."
- **5.3 The gaps (examples — felt vignettes, NO apps named):**
  1. Your close rate could be higher and you can't see exactly why.
  2. Leads slipping away — slow follow-up, no follow-up, or outreach that won't scale.
  3. Flying blind on your own numbers — no clear read on where revenue actually stands.
  4. Content and marketing output that's a fraction of what it should be.
  5. Paying people, or spend, for work a system could just… do.
- **5.4 The live poll** — "Drop in the chat: which of these is most you right now? I'm genuinely curious how this room splits."
- **5.5 The takeaway (not-a-menu widen)** — "You don't need all of these. And if your real gap isn't even one I listed — fine. The point isn't the list. Wherever your revenue engine leaks, the right system can fix it. In a minute I'll take each of these and show you exactly how." *(Scope discipline: revenue/sales/marketing, NOT "any business problem.")*
- **Private mapping (NOT spoken — pairs each gap to its deep-dive machine):** gap 1 → Call Grader · gap 2 → DealForge / Automated SDR · gap 3 → Revenue Dashboard · gap 4 → CreativeIQ · gap 5 → cross-cutting cost angle.

### 5B. QUICK CTA + TRANSITION *(new — slots between the gaps sub-agenda and the deep-dive; gets a real section number when the back half is renumbered)*
*Purpose: capture the already-convinced now (they just heard a gap that's them), give the "some of you have to jump" crowd a clean exit + booking, then transition the rest into the deep-dive.*
- **5B.1 The quick CTA** — "If one of those just landed — if you felt 'yeah, that's me' — you don't have to wait until the end. The link's on screen; put a call in now while it's fresh, and we'll dig into your specific gaps one-on-one."
- **5B.2 The 'some of you have to jump' line** — "And look, I know some of you can't stay the whole time. Totally fine — if that's you, book the call and jump; you'll get the personal version of all of this. For the rest of you who are curious — let's actually go into each of these, one at a time."
- **5B.3 Transition** — hand straight into the deep-dive: each gap + the machine that fixes it, a couple of minutes each.

### 6. SYSTEM FRAME — 10,000-ft view (the METHOD)
*Purpose: give the lens that makes every use case make sense; frame tools as a system.*
- **6.1 The core principle** — AI's power isn't doing what you do slightly cheaper; it's doing the *valuable work you couldn't do before* (no time / no people / no consistency): personalization at scale, grading every call, following up every lead.
- **6.2 The reframe question** — not "where do I bolt on a chatbot" but *"where in my revenue engine is valuable work not getting done because no human has the time?"* That's where the machines go.
- **6.3 The method (4 beats)** — **Diagnose → Build → Adopt → Compound.** *(Open: surface on a slide vs. keep implicit — lean surface.)*
- **6.4 Mid-CTA #1 (soft)** — "want to know which machines your business needs? I'll show you how to get a free, specific answer at the end. For now — let's see the machines."

### 7. THE MACHINE WALK — deep dive = the proof (6 use cases)
*Purpose: make them want a specific machine they can picture in their business.*
*Per-machine 4-beat pattern: valuable-work-not-getting-done → the machine → what it does → the result/dogfounding proof.*
- **7.1 Call Grader** — nobody reviews every call (no time); grades 100% vs. your framework, surfaces where each rep leaks deals. *(Tie: doubled close rate.)*
- **7.2 DealForge** — auto-builds the post-call asset package (recap, tailored proposal, objection doc, matched case studies) from the transcript. *(Honesty: "built & running on my own pipeline.")* *(Tie: speed-to-close.)*
- **7.3 Mid-CTA #2 (soft, planted)** — "want to know which of these *your* business needs? Keep watching — I'll show you how at the end."
- **7.4 Automated SDR** — genuine per-prospect research + personalized outreach at a volume no human team sustains; never a slow week.
- **7.5 CreativeIQ** — monitors competitors' live ads/content, extracts working angles, turns them into your copy & scripts. Content-team output without the team.
- **7.6 Revenue Dashboard** — real-time picture of where deals stall / stages bleed / what closes this month. Can't fix what you can't see.
- **7.7 Persistent Agent Layer** — sits across all of it, watches the revenue op continuously, flags issues before they bite; turns tools into a system. *(Tension C — naming.)*
- **7.8 The unifier (critical line)** — "These six are examples, not a menu. At the core: we find where your revenue engine leaks and build AI machines to plug it. The specific machines depend on *your* gaps." *(Resolves deliverable-ambiguity live; sets up the audit.)*

### 8. SUMMARY OF PROCESS — zoom back out
*Purpose: recap the method so it reads as a repeatable system, not a demo reel.*
- **8.1 The method recap** — Diagnose → Build → Adopt → Compound, now seen through the machines they just watched.
- **8.2 The compounding promise** — every machine added → more leverage per person → **revenue per employee & profit per employee climb** (the quantified "1 does the work of 10"). *(Land it here — most founder-resonant line.)*
- **8.3 Mid-CTA #3** — transition into the offer.

### 9. THE OFFER — who we are + the engagement model
*Purpose: name the model; make the embedded-operator relationship feel like relief, not lock-in.*
- **9.1 The model** — fractional **CAIRO**: a senior operator who owns the AI side of your revenue engine; an extension of your team, not a vendor you manage.
- **9.2 What we do** — assess the gaps, build the machines/infra/agents/workflows, train your team so it sticks.
- **9.3 Why ongoing (the divergence)** — QS says "not an agency, you own it." **RevenueForge is the opposite — an ongoing embedded operator.** Logistics must justify why: continuous diagnosis, machine #2/#3, the system keeps compounding. *(Tension D.)*

### 11. COST OF INACTION
*Purpose: reframe risk from "what if this fails" to "what if I stay where I am."*
- **11.1 Competitors move first** — deploy AI revenue systems, reach your market, build trust, close before you're in the conversation. Advantage compounds; not early = late.
- **11.2 You stay stuck** — AI toys + the same channels that already underperform.
- **11.3 The real cost is time** — yours, spent on manual work instead of the business.
- **11.4 The reframe** — "Not 'should I do this' — but 'how long can you afford not to?'"

# RevenueForge — Cold Calendar Outreach Target List

*Decided 2026-08-25. Governs list-building for the cold calendar invite → webinar → $950 audit funnel.*

**Scope:** US only · $3M hard floor, $5–20M core, $25M soft ceiling · founder-led B2B
**Mechanism:** cold calendar invite (not a registration email) → webinar → paid $950 Revenue Audit
**Wave 1 is ordered by strength of fit, not list volume** — agencies are the biggest list, enterprise software the best-converting.

**Governing ruling (Lloyd, 2026-08-25):** the **$3M revenue floor governs**. The "B2B SaaS (seed–Series A)" label in the Brain ICP page (`revenueforge_icp`) is STALE — seed-to-Series-A B2B SaaS sits mostly under the floor. Do not build lists off that label.

---

## Wave 1 — send first

### 1. Enterprise / mid-market-selling B2B software, $25k+ ACV
Vertical SaaS (construction, healthcare RCM, logistics, legal, fintech infra), compliance & security platforms, data/analytics platforms, industrial & supply-chain software.
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Computer Software, Internet · **Headcount:** 25–150
- **Why:** High ACV means a close-rate point is worth real money. Long consultative cycles make this DealForge's strongest match on the whole list. Salesforce/HubSpot always present and populated — cleanest audit scan available. Founder usually came up through sales, so a talk about close rates is something they'd actually attend.
- **Catch:** Enterprise sales orgs professionalize ops early, so the no-RevOps filter bites hardest here. Sweet spot is **$3–10M**, not the full band.

**Filter recipe (both employment signals required):**
- **Current employees holding AE titles** — 3+ people with Account Executive, Enterprise AE, Strategic AE, or Mid-Market AE. Proves the sales team exists now and is the shape the machines act on.
- **Open job postings for AE roles** — live reqs for AE/BD/Enterprise AE. Proves expansion, and collapses the "hiring the 3rd+ rep" trigger into the base filter for this segment.
- **No public pricing page** — "Request a Demo" / "Contact Sales" primary CTA with no pricing is the best single tell they sell through humans. Inverse filter: "start free" / "free trial" = PLG, drop it.
- **An SDR/BDR function exists** — implies outbound sales-led.
- **Vertical SaaS keyword** — "software for [industry]" in the description; vertical SaaS is almost always sales-led.

### 2. CRM implementation partners — Salesforce, HubSpot, NetSuite, ServiceNow
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** IT & Services, Computer Software · **Headcount:** 20–120
- **Why:** Their whole business runs inside a CRM, so the scan is clean day one. They sell projects on discovery calls with real BD teams. They already know what a revenue system is, which shortens the sale.
- **Catch:** The most credible group to turn around and resell what you show them.

### 3. B2B marketing / creative / performance agencies
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Marketing & Advertising, Marketing Services · **Headcount:** 20–120
- **Why:** Deal-based, relationship-heavy, usually spreadsheet-managed. Founder still pitches. Proposal and follow-up burn is enormous — DealForge lands with no translation. Biggest list volume of any segment.
- **Catch:** Most likely to copy what you demo. Margin-sensitive.

### 4. Management, ops & specialist consultancies
Strategy, operations/supply-chain, HR & people-ops, cybersecurity/compliance, accounting advisory (CAS practices), and **fractional-exec placement firms** (Chief Outsiders, larger CFO practice groups, bigger EOS implementer orgs — the firms that *place* execs, not individuals doing the work).
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Management Consulting, Business Consulting · **Headcount:** 20–120
- **Why:** Same motion as agencies, higher ACV, far less likely to resell.
- **Catch:** Fragmented, hard to see the sales team from outside. "Consulting" in Apollo mostly returns sub-$3M boutiques — filter hard.

### 5. MSPs / IT services / managed security
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** IT & Services, Computer & Network Security · **Headcount:** 20–120
- **Why:** Recurring contracts, real BD teams, CRM always present (ConnectWise, HubSpot). Technical about IT, completely non-technical about revenue ops.
- **Catch:** Will say "we're technical." Weaker than the SaaS version — their engineers are billable, not free.

### 6. Recruiting & staffing (client-side BD)
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Staffing & Recruiting · **Headcount:** 15–100 internal
- **Why:** BD is pure call-based. Follow-up is relentless and manual. Founder-led at this size. Always a CRM/ATS. Client-acquisition half only — candidate sourcing is a different problem and not in scope.
- **Catch:** The CRM is the ATS (Bullhorn, JobAdder) with candidate and client data tangled. Heavily marketed-to inbox.

### 7. Wealth management / RIAs
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Financial Services, Investment Management · **Headcount:** 10–60
- **Why:** Most webinar-native segment on the list — they sell their own clients by seminar, so the format reads professional. Highest LTV per client. Principal-led, genuinely non-technical. **Calls are already recorded by regulation**, so the transcripts Sales Pulse needs already exist.
- **Catch:** The ICP doc has them in Phase 2 — not for compliance reasons but because their gaps may not *rhyme* with agencies', which fragments the standardized audit and costs margin. Advisors are often 1099, so grading their calls is a political problem, not a technical one.
- **Position change (2026-08-25):** promoted from Wave 2. Compliance was over-weighted — the first two machines (Revenue Dashboard, Sales Pulse) are internal-facing; exposure sits on the client-facing AI SDR, which is machine four.

### 8. Tech-enabled services (software layer + service delivery)
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** IT & Services, Internet, Outsourcing · **Headcount:** 25–150
- **Why:** Named in the ICP doc's own Phase 1. Hybrid pipeline complexity nobody has solved for them.
- **Catch:** A business model, not an Apollo industry — found by keyword and site-read. Slowest list to build.

## Wave 2 — run alongside, smaller volume, expect different show rates

### 9. Bootstrapped / founder-led B2B software, $3M+ ARR (the non-enterprise-selling remainder)
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Computer Software, Internet · **Headcount:** 25–150
- **Why:** Acute pipeline pain, high CRM adoption.
- **Catch:** Most saturated cold inbox anywhere, lowest webinar attendance, "we'll build it." The funded cohort fails the no-RevOps filter automatically.

### 10. Insurance brokerages & mortgage brokerages
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Insurance, Financial Services · **Headcount:** 10–60
- **Why:** Call-based, principal-led, same follow-up gap as wealth management.
- **Catch:** Rate-driven and cyclical. Mortgage in particular is volume-over-value, which weakens the price justification.

## Wave 3 — hold until the webinar is converting

### 11. B2B manufacturing & distribution
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Machinery, Industrial Automation, Wholesale, Building Materials · **Headcount:** 15–80
- **Why:** Long cycles, complex quoting, strong ROI, and an inbox nobody is competing for.
- **Catch:** Least webinar-native of everyone. CRM often absent → degraded audit (Engine B only).

### 12. Healthcare services & medtech, B2B-selling only
- **Apollo/SalesNav:** Hospital & Health Care, Medical Devices · **Headcount:** 20–100
- **Why:** Relationship-driven; compliance forces process adoption.
- **Catch:** Only those selling *to* practices or systems qualify. Compliance overhead.

### 13. PE-backed platform companies, 0–12 months post-acquisition, founder still in seat
- **Why:** Structural urgency from the exit horizon, budget already allocated, founder still owns the number — the window before the CRO and RevOps hires land.
- **Catch:** A **continuous trigger list**, not an industry — built from public acquisition announcements, re-pulled monthly. Short window. Don't attempt at launch.

## Channel, not invite list

These convert on a relationship sale to one person, not a cold webinar. Different motion, different copy, worth more than a single client each.

- **Individual and boutique fractional execs** — too small for the floor, but they sit inside their clients' operations and get asked about AI constantly with no answer.
- **PE and VC firms themselves** — a firm with twelve portfolio companies mandating ops improvement is distribution into twelve ICP-perfect companies at once. Sell to the operating partner. As direct clients they fail: partnership decision-making (no founder owning revenue), revenue is carry/fees so "revenue leaking" doesn't parse, 12–36 month deal cycles outrun any pilot, near-zero cold-webinar attendance, and the dogfounding proof arc reads small to them.

## Filters that apply to every list above

These do more work than the industry choice does.

- **US only.**
- **3+ people carrying sales titles** — AE, BD, SDR, Account Manager, Sales Director.
- **Founder / CEO / Owner / Managing Partner present and not insulated** — no CRO between them and revenue.
- **Exclude any company with a VP or Head of RevOps, Sales Ops, Growth Ops, or an internal AI/automation title.** The ICP doc calls this the core fit signal.
- **Exclude 40+ person revenue orgs** — the lean-leverage story stops landing.
- **Target title:** Founder, CEO, Co-Founder, President, Owner, Managing Partner. Fall back to VP Sales / Head of Sales only where the founder isn't reachable.

---

## Explicitly out

- Solo and boutique consultants — below floor, and most of what "consulting" returns.
- Engineering, environmental and architectural consultancies — right size, wrong motion; they win through RFPs, not sales calls, so three of the four hero demos don't land.
- Coaches, course creators, info products, sub-$3M consultancies — QS's floor, not RevenueForge's. The easiest list to accidentally reuse.
- B2C and transactional ecommerce — excluded outright in the ICP doc.
- Local and home services, med spas, dental practices.
- Corporate divisions — the founder isn't the buyer.

## Trigger overlay

Ranks the lists, doesn't replace them. Any record matching one jumps the queue. All four are from the ICP doc's own "Outreach Targeting Triggers".

- **Hiring the 3rd+ sales rep** — live AE/BD/SDR job postings. Already baked into segment 1's base filter.
- **Recently promoted an IC to sales manager** — LinkedIn title change. The ICP doc calls this the strongest trigger: personally exposed, has budget, managing by gut and knows it.
- **Sales headcount grew, company revenue didn't.**
- **Has AI or automation tools in the stack but no RevOps hire** — the ICP doc flags this as the fastest close.

## Operating notes

- **Tag the vertical on every invite.** Running all waves at once costs nothing extra on list acquisition, but without a vertical tag the show rate and audit-purchase rate come back unattributable and the test teaches nothing.
- **One webinar holds segments 1–6 and 8** — close enough buyer psychology. **7 and 11 diverge**: wealth management has a compliance-and-1099 frame, manufacturing a quoting-and-cycle frame, and the current webinar speaks to neither.
- **CRM is not a hard gate.** The ICP doc: an existing CRM, even underused or untrusted, is a positive signal. The audit has two engines — the automated CRM scan (Engine A) and the operator-led strategic diagnostic (Engine B). No CRM = a weaker audit, not no audit.
